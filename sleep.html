Sleep

Sleep supplements are among the best-studied categories in the industry — but not all popular ingredients are backed equally.

Reviewed for medical accuracy · Health Editorial Team
Updated May 2026
7 min read

Quick answer: Melatonin has the strongest and most consistent evidence, particularly for shifting sleep timing (jet lag, shift work). Magnesium and L-theanine have solid supporting data for sleep quality and relaxation. Valerian root and chamomile have weaker, more mixed evidence despite widespread traditional use.

Most people treat poor sleep as something to medicate away, when it's often the last symptom of something else worth understanding first.

Overview

Poor sleep affects nearly every aspect of health, from cognitive performance to metabolism and immune function. Sleep supplements range from well-studied hormones like melatonin to traditional herbal remedies with much thinner clinical backing.

What Affects Sleep Quality

  • Irregular sleep schedule — inconsistent bed and wake times disrupt the body's internal circadian clock.
  • Screen exposure at night — blue light exposure before bed can delay natural melatonin release.
  • Caffeine and stimulants — even afternoon caffeine intake can measurably reduce sleep quality that night.
  • Stress and racing thoughts — elevated cortisol and mental activity make it harder to fall and stay asleep.
  • Underlying sleep disorders — conditions like sleep apnea or restless leg syndrome are common and often undiagnosed.

Common Symptoms

  • Difficulty falling asleep
  • Frequent waking during the night
  • Waking up feeling unrefreshed
  • Daytime fatigue or sleepiness
  • Irregular or inconsistent sleep patterns

Lifestyle & Nutrition

A consistent sleep schedule, limiting screens and caffeine in the evening, keeping the bedroom cool and dark, and regular daytime exercise all have strong evidence for improving sleep quality — often more reliably than any single supplement.

Dosage & Side Effects

Typical doses reflect amounts commonly used in clinical research — always follow the label on the specific product you're using, and check with a doctor if you're on medication.

Valerian Root300–600 mg before bedMorning grogginess in some users; avoid combining with sedatives

Who Should Use This — and Who Shouldn't

✅ Generally a good fit for

  • Adults with occasional difficulty falling asleep
  • Those adjusting to a new time zone or shift schedule
  • People whose sleep issues are linked to stress or racing thoughts

⚠️ Best to avoid or check with a doctor first

  • Anyone on sedative medication (risk of excess drowsiness)
  • Pregnant or nursing individuals without medical guidance
  • Those needing to be alert shortly after waking (next-day grogginess risk)

When to See a Doctor

See a doctor if poor sleep persists for more than a few weeks despite lifestyle changes, or if you experience loud snoring, gasping during sleep, or excessive daytime sleepiness — these can indicate sleep apnea or another disorder that supplements won't resolve.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is melatonin safe to take every night?

Short-term use is generally well tolerated, but it's best used at the lowest effective dose and discussed with a doctor for long-term use.

Why do valerian root products vary so much in effectiveness?

Preparations differ widely in concentration and standardization, which contributes to the inconsistent trial results.

Can magnesium help with sleep even without a deficiency?

Most of the sleep-related research is strongest in people with lower magnesium status, though some benefit is seen more broadly.

How much melatonin should I take?

Clinical trials often use doses far lower (0.5–3mg) than what's sold in stores — more isn't necessarily better for sleep onset.

🔑 Key Takeaways

  • Melatonin's strongest evidence is for shifting sleep timing, not just "more sleep" in general.
  • Clinical trial doses of melatonin are often lower than what's sold in stores.
  • Magnesium and L-theanine both have solid evidence for relaxation and sleep quality.
  • A consistent schedule and less screen time at night still outperform most supplements.
