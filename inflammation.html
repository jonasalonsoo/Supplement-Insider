Inflammation

Chronic low-grade inflammation is linked to a wide range of health issues. Here's what the research says about the most common anti-inflammatory ingredients.

Medical Editor: Health Editorial Team
Updated May 2026
8 min read

Quick answer: Curcumin and omega-3 fatty acids have the strongest and most consistent clinical evidence for reducing markers of inflammation. Boswellia and ginger have moderate supporting data. Ingredients like resveratrol and bromelain are popular but have weaker human trial evidence.

Inflammation isn't the enemy — chronic, low-grade inflammation that never turns off is. Knowing the difference changes what's worth taking.

Overview

Inflammation is a normal, necessary immune response — but when it becomes chronic and low-grade, it's been linked in research to a wide range of conditions, from joint pain to cardiovascular and metabolic issues. Several dietary compounds have been studied specifically for their effect on inflammatory markers.

What Drives Chronic Inflammation

  • Diet high in ultra-processed food — diets high in refined sugar and processed fats are consistently linked to higher inflammatory markers.
  • Excess body fat — fat tissue, particularly around the abdomen, actively produces inflammatory signaling molecules.
  • Chronic stress — prolonged stress hormone elevation is associated with increased systemic inflammation.
  • Poor sleep — insufficient sleep is linked to elevated inflammatory markers in multiple studies.
  • Sedentary lifestyle — regular physical activity is one of the most consistent inflammation-lowering interventions.

Common Symptoms

  • Persistent joint or muscle aches
  • Fatigue that doesn't improve with rest
  • Digestive discomfort
  • Skin issues like redness or breakouts
  • General feeling of being run down

Lifestyle & Nutrition

An anti-inflammatory dietary pattern — rich in vegetables, fatty fish, olive oil, and fiber, and lower in refined sugar and processed food — has some of the strongest evidence for reducing chronic inflammation. Regular exercise, adequate sleep, and stress management all measurably lower inflammatory markers as well.

Here's how commonly used anti-inflammatory ingredients compare on the evidence.

Who Should Use This — and Who Shouldn't

✅ Generally a good fit for

  • Adults managing occasional joint or muscle inflammation
  • Those following an anti-inflammatory diet looking for additional support
  • People without a bleeding disorder or blood-thinner prescription

⚠️ Best to avoid or check with a doctor first

  • Anyone on blood-thinning medication without medical guidance
  • Those with gallbladder issues (curcumin can stimulate bile production)
  • Pregnant or nursing individuals without medical advice

When to See a Doctor

See a doctor for inflammation accompanied by unexplained weight loss, persistent fever, or symptoms lasting more than a few weeks — these may indicate an underlying condition, such as an autoimmune disorder, that requires medical evaluation and treatment beyond diet or supplements.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you test your own inflammation levels?

Blood tests like CRP (C-reactive protein) can give a general marker of systemic inflammation, though they don't diagnose a specific cause.

Is curcumin better absorbed with black pepper?

Piperine (from black pepper) has been shown to significantly increase curcumin absorption in several studies.

Can diet alone control chronic inflammation?

For many people, diet and lifestyle changes have a larger and more sustainable effect than any single supplement.

Are anti-inflammatory supplements safe long-term?

Most, like curcumin and omega-3s, have good long-term safety data, though very high doses of curcumin can interact with blood thinners.

🔑 Key Takeaways

  • Curcumin and omega-3s are two of the best-evidenced anti-inflammatory ingredients available.
  • An anti-inflammatory diet pattern often matters more than any single supplement.
  • Chronic inflammation lasting weeks with no clear cause deserves medical evaluation.
  • Piperine (black pepper extract) meaningfully boosts curcumin absorption.
