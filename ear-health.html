Ear Health

Tinnitus and hearing-related supplements are widely marketed despite a genuinely thin evidence base. Here's an honest look at what's been studied.

Reviewed by our Medical Review Board
Updated May 2026
7 min read

Quick answer: No supplement has strong, consistent evidence for reducing tinnitus in the general population. Zinc and vitamin B12 may help specifically in people who are deficient. Melatonin has moderate evidence for improving sleep quality in people whose tinnitus disrupts sleep, which is a meaningfully different outcome than curing tinnitus itself.

Tinnitus is one of the most heavily marketed — and least understood — categories in the entire supplement industry.

Overview

Tinnitus — a ringing, buzzing, or hissing sound not caused by an external source — affects a significant portion of adults, especially with age or noise exposure. Despite a large market of "tinnitus relief" supplements, the underlying evidence is notably weaker than in most other supplement categories.

What Causes Tinnitus

  • Noise-induced hearing damage — long-term exposure to loud noise is one of the most common identifiable causes.
  • Age-related hearing loss — gradual hearing decline with age is frequently accompanied by tinnitus.
  • Earwax blockage — in some cases, simple earwax buildup can cause or worsen tinnitus symptoms.
  • Certain medications — some medications list tinnitus as a potential side effect, particularly at high doses.
  • Underlying conditions — circulatory issues, TMJ disorders, or Meniere's disease can all contribute to tinnitus.

Common Symptoms

  • Ringing, buzzing, or hissing sounds
  • Sounds that worsen in quiet environments
  • Difficulty concentrating due to persistent noise
  • Sleep disruption from tinnitus sounds
  • Sounds that fluctuate with stress or fatigue

Lifestyle & Nutrition

Protecting hearing from loud noise exposure (using earplugs, lowering volume) is the most important preventive measure. Managing stress, limiting caffeine, and improving sleep hygiene can reduce how disruptive tinnitus feels, even when the underlying sound doesn't change.

Who Should Use This — and Who Shouldn't

✅ Generally a good fit for

  • Adults whose tinnitus is disrupting sleep specifically
  • Those with a confirmed zinc or magnesium deficiency
  • People seeking general ear-health support alongside hearing protection habits

⚠️ Best to avoid or check with a doctor first

  • Anyone expecting a supplement to eliminate the tinnitus sound itself
  • Those on blood-thinning medication (ginkgo)
  • Pregnant or nursing individuals without medical guidance

When to See a Doctor

New or sudden tinnitus, especially in one ear, or tinnitus accompanied by hearing loss or dizziness, should be evaluated by a doctor promptly — it can occasionally signal an underlying condition that needs specific treatment, and an audiologist can also help rule out treatable causes like earwax buildup.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does ginkgo biloba really help tinnitus?

Despite being one of the most marketed tinnitus ingredients, most large, well-designed trials have not shown a consistent benefit over placebo.

Can tinnitus be cured with supplements?

There's no supplement with strong evidence for curing tinnitus; management typically focuses on reducing how disruptive it feels.

Why is melatonin included in tinnitus formulas if it doesn't reduce the sound?

Because tinnitus often disrupts sleep, and improving sleep quality can meaningfully reduce how distressing the condition feels overall.

Should I get a hearing test if I have tinnitus?

Yes — tinnitus and hearing loss are closely linked, and a hearing evaluation can help identify an underlying, treatable cause.

🔑 Key Takeaways

  • Ginkgo biloba, despite heavy marketing, hasn't shown consistent benefit for tinnitus in large trials.
  • Melatonin doesn't reduce tinnitus itself but can meaningfully improve related sleep disruption.
  • Zinc and B12 mainly help if you're actually deficient in them.
  • New or sudden tinnitus deserves a hearing evaluation, not just supplements.
